Sofra · UX & Service Design · Sustainability & FoodTech · 2025

Designing a second life for food.

Sofra means table in Turkish , a place where people gather, share, and have a good time. Sofra is a community-driven platform that gives surplus food a second life by connecting restaurants, volunteers, and consumers in one seamless experience.

The problem wasn't just food waste. It was:

How might we make food rescue, volunteering, and social impact part of a single, engaging everyday experience?

THE PROBLEM

Food isn't wasted because nobody cares. It's wasted because there's no easy way to act.

A significant share of edible food goes to waste from restaurants, grocery stores, and food service providers every day. Existing solutions focus almost entirely on selling surplus food at discounted prices , which addresses consumption but ignores community.

There's a gap between food rescue efforts and community engagement. Many people are willing to contribute, but they lack a clear and accessible way to participate. Sofra is designed to close that gap.

WHO IT'S FOR

Three different people, one shared goal.

Sofra brings together three distinct users whose needs are deeply connected , and whose participation makes the system work.

01

The volunteer

"I have some free time. I want to find meaningful volunteering opportunities nearby , without committing to something long-term."

02

The student

"I want an affordable meal. I want to discover surplus food nearby so I can save money while reducing food waste."

03

The restaurant

"When we have surplus food, we want an easy way to redistribute it , and make use of meals that would otherwise go to waste."

HOW IT WORKS

A loop where everyone benefits.

Instead of letting perfectly edible food go to waste, restaurants list surplus meals at reduced prices. Volunteers help distribute food to local community organizations , and earn credits in return. Those credits can be redeemed for discounted meals, or donated to support others.

TYPOGRAPHY

Type as the voice of the system.

Before screens, there is type. Sofra's typography system was established first , defining the tone, hierarchy, and personality that would carry through every surface of the experience.

RESEARCH

Starting with the right questions.

Before designing, I needed to understand what was actually missing. Interviews helped uncover why existing food rescue solutions weren't engaging people , and what it would take to change that.

WIREFRAMES

Mapping the experience before building it.

Wireframes translated research insights into structure , testing the logic of the rescue flow, the credit system, and the community features before any visual decisions were made.

ONBOARDING & VOLUNTEER GOALS

First impressions that set the right expectations.

Onboarding does more than introduce the app , it establishes what Sofra stands for and what role the user plays in it. The goal was to make the value proposition clear before asking for any commitment.

From there, participants are invited to set their own volunteering goals , deciding how often they want to contribute, what kind of rescues they want to take on, and what they're working toward. Setting intentions early gives users a personal stake in the experience before their first rescue even begins.

HOME & COMMUNITIES

Find a cause, leave a rescue.

The home screen is where everything begins , nearby rescues, weekly impact, and a clear path to the communities that need them most. Once a user picks up a rescue, they can browse Sofra's partner organizations, choose a cause they care about, and drop off their rescue there. The act of giving becomes intentional, not incidental.

GOALS, BADGES & ACHIEVEMENTS

Turning commitment into something worth celebrating.

Every rescue completed brings users closer to their weekly goal , and every milestone earns them a badge that reflects the kind of contributor they've become. The achievement system is designed to make consistency feel rewarding, not obligatory. Progress is visible, personal, and worth coming back for.

USE OR DONATE CREDITS

Your credits, your choice.

After completing a rescue, users earn credits they can do something meaningful with. They can browse available surplus meals and redeem their credits for a discounted meal , or choose to donate them to a community organization that needs the support. Either way, the loop closes with an act of care.

BRANDING

A visual identity that feels like a table worth sitting at.

The brand brings the system together , rooted in warmth, community, and the idea of shared nourishment. Every visual choice reflects what it feels like to be part of something that matters.

THE TAKEAWAY
Sustainable behavior doesn't need to feel like sacrifice.

This project taught me that the barrier to sustainable action is rarely motivation , it's friction. When volunteering is nearby, low-commitment, and visibly rewarding, people participate.

Sofra explores how thoughtful UX and service design can transform an everyday decision , what to eat, how to spend an afternoon , into a small act of collective care.
